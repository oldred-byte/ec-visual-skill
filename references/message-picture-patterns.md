# Message-to-Picture Patterns

## Purpose

Most e-commerce messages fall into a small number of recurring categories. Each category has a picture pattern that has been proven to make the message obvious without copy. Use this library as the first lookup when planning any screen — adapt the pattern to the actual product and category, do not copy literally.

This library is the most direct cure for "图不达意 / picture doesn't convey the message". When the model picks a pattern from this library, it gets a concrete picture (not an abstract proof-form label) and a product-presence rule (no packaging by default for most patterns).

## How To Use

For each planned output:

1. Identify the screen's core message.
2. Find the matching pattern(s) below.
3. Adapt the picture to the actual product, ingredient, mechanism, or scene.
4. Apply the product-presence rule — if the pattern says "no packaging", the packaging does NOT appear, even partially.
5. Write the Picture Solo Statement based on the adapted picture.

If a message does not fit any pattern, design a new picture using the Picture Solo Test in `visual-proof-grammar.md`. Do not fall back to product-hero.

## Pattern Library

### 1. Multi-flavor / Multi-color / Multi-Variant

**Message shape**: "Available in 3 flavors / 5 colors / multiple variants / N kinds."

**Picture**: The actual contents arranged in a clean row or grid. Each variant gets its own position. Shot from above or 3/4 angle. Background is a clean, neutral surface in the brand/product palette.

**Examples**:
- Baby food puree: three bowls of puree — banana (left), blueberry (center), oat (right) — top-down, cream-colored surface.
- Yarn / textile: five yarn balls or fabric swatches in a color spectrum.
- Skincare: three bottles of different serum colors in a row, each tint visible.

**Product presence**: No packaging in the main visual. The contents ARE the proof. The packaging may appear as a small corner element only when the user needs variant recognition, and even then, optional.

**Anti-template**: Do NOT show the same pouch/bottle three times with different labels. The picture proves the message by showing the actual different contents, not by labeling.

### 2. Natural / Simple / Clean Ingredients

**Message shape**: "Only 3 ingredients / no additives / clean formula / simple composition."

**Picture**: The raw ingredients arranged as a flat-lay or still life on a clean surface. Each ingredient is recognizable. No industrial-style graphics, no lab icons. The simplicity of the arrangement IS the proof.

**Examples**:
- Baby food: three raw ingredients — a banana, a handful of blueberries, oat grains — on a cream linen.
- Skincare: a single bottle surrounded by the 3-4 raw botanicals that went into it.
- Seasoning / sauce: the source ingredients laid out as a still life.

**Product presence**: No packaging. The ingredients carry the message.

**Anti-template**: Do NOT add "0 additive" pictograms, "✓ no preservative" checklists, or lab-style infographics. Those are icon-system violations and they actually weaken the proof.

### 3. Soft / Skin-Feel / Material Feel

**Message shape**: "Soft / gentle / silky / cushioning / skin-friendly feel."

**Picture**: A macro of the material or texture, often with a body contact point (fingertip, cheek, forearm) interacting with it. The light reveals surface texture. The body part is in a relaxed, natural posture.

**Examples**:
- Towel / fabric: a fingertip pressing into a fabric fold, soft directional light.
- Skincare: a drop of serum on a fingertip near a cheek, macro.
- Pillow / cushion: a hand pressing into the cushion surface, depression visible.
- Razors: a single blade gliding along a jawline curve, no full product visible.

**Product presence**: No packaging. Often no full product either — only the material or the contact detail.

**Anti-template**: Do NOT add a "soft" icon (cloud, feather, pillow pictogram). The macro IS the proof.

### 4. Micro-Material / Texture / Detail Structure

**Message shape**: "Microscopic structure / weave density / surface engineering / internal composition."

**Picture**: Extreme macro of the relevant material. Often a single detail that reveals how the product is constructed. Lighting reveals micro-texture.

**Examples**:
- Razor blade: macro of blade edge and mesh.
- Fabric: macro of weave structure.
- Skincare: macro of cream texture / droplet.
- Hardware / tech product: macro of finish, port, or mechanism.

**Product presence**: No packaging. Often no recognizable product silhouette either — the macro detail is the entire frame.

**Anti-template**: Do NOT add magnifying-glass icons, "high-precision" labels, or technical-readout graphics. The macro itself carries the message.

### 5. Mechanism / Technology / Structure

**Message shape**: "How it works / the technology inside / the structural innovation."

**Picture**: A visual explanation of the mechanism: cross-section, exploded view, energy path, signal path, or labeled structure. Can be a controlled render or a diagrammatic photograph.

**Examples**:
- Earbuds: exploded view of driver, battery, antenna, with subtle path lines (text labels allowed if minimal).
- Skincare: penetration/absorption visualized as layered skin cross-section.
- Detergent: stain-lifting mechanism shown as before/after cross-section.
- Shaver: internal motor and blade assembly cutaway.

**Product presence**: Mechanism-led, NOT full-product hero. The product may be cutaway, exploded, or rendered semi-transparent.

**Anti-template**: Do NOT add gears / lightning / signal icons. The mechanism rendering IS the proof. Labels are allowed when they identify real visible parts.

### 6. Scene-Fit / Lifestyle / Audience Identification

**Message shape**: "Made for [audience] / fits [scene] / belongs in [moment]."

**Picture**: A real-feel scene where the audience recognizes their life. The product may appear naturally placed in the scene, but the scene relationship is the main subject.

**Examples**:
- Baby food: a parent holding a baby at the table, baby food moment.
- Skincare: a person at their bathroom sink in the morning, product in use.
- Earbuds: a commuter on a train, wearing the earbuds.
- Coffee: a workdesk with a laptop and a cup.

**Product presence**: Small context, or in-use medium. Often the scene is wider than the product.

**Anti-template**: Do NOT use grid of 4 persona cards with icons. Use one credible scene per screen instead.

### 7. Operation / How-To-Use

**Message shape**: "How to use / one-step operation / easy open-close / setup in seconds."

**Picture**: The actual moment of operation: hand opening, closing, pressing, rotating, applying. The action is captured mid-motion. Often sequential split-frame or before/after.

**Examples**:
- Baby food pouch: hand twisting the cap off.
- Earbuds: hand opening the charging case lid.
- Skincare: hand pressing the pump.
- Appliance: finger pressing the power button.

**Product presence**: Medium product in use. Often partial product visible (the part being operated).

**Anti-template**: Do NOT add "step 1 / step 2 / step 3" icon sequences with arrows. Show the actual action.

### 8. Sensory Result / Taste / Skin Result

**Message shape**: "Tastes like X / feels like Y on skin / leaves skin Z."

**Picture**: The result state itself, often on a body part or in a vessel. The visible result IS the proof.

**Examples**:
- Baby food: a baby's mouth mid-taste, expression of acceptance, or a clean spoon with food texture.
- Skincare: a cheek surface with visible glow/hydration.
- Beverage: a glass with visible layering or fizz.
- Hair care: a strand of hair with visible shine.

**Product presence**: No packaging. Often no full body either — the result detail (mouth, cheek, strand, surface) is the entire frame.

**Anti-template**: Do NOT add "tastes great" or "feels soft" icons. The visible result carries the message.

### 9. Parameter / Spec / Detail

**Message shape**: "Specific dimension / capacity / weight / count / material spec."

**Picture**: A clean composition that makes the parameter visually obvious: scale comparison, dimension lines (text labels allowed), or a parameter-organized layout.

**Examples**:
- Battery / capacity: the product next to a familiar reference (hand, coin, fruit) for scale.
- Dimensions: a flat technical drawing with measurement lines, monospace typography.
- Material spec: the cross-section of the material with thickness labeled.

**Product presence**: Often partial product crop, or product next to scale reference.

**Anti-template**: Do NOT use big spec-card grids with icons. Use one clean composition that makes the parameter obvious.

### 10. Trust / Quality Basis

**Message shape**: "Tested / inspected / safe / durable / quality you can trust."

**Picture**: A scene or moment that conveys inspection, testing, or craftsmanship without inventing certifications. Lab-like environment, controlled light, focused attention on the product or material.

**Examples**:
- Baby food: a clean inspection environment, the product being checked (without fake certification labels).
- Tech product: a stress test or drop test moment.
- Textile: a tensile strength or wear test.
- Skincare: a dermatologist-style scene (without inventing doctor names or certifications).

**Product presence**: Variable. The inspection/testing moment is the proof, not the product hero.

**Anti-template**: Do NOT add fake certification seals, official partner logos, award ribbons, or star ratings. Those are claim-safety violations.

### 11. Closing Set / Brand Memory

**Message shape**: "The full set / brand world / final buying reason."

**Picture**: A still-life of the product family arranged as a coherent set. Premium light, controlled shadow, sparse copy. This is one of the few screens where packaging appearance is the point.

**Examples**:
- Baby food: the full pouch range arranged as a family.
- Skincare: the full routine set arranged on a vanity.
- Earbuds: the case + earbuds + accessory box arranged as a product family.

**Product presence**: Full product hero / set display. This is where packaging earns its place.

**Anti-template**: Do NOT add icon "checklist of benefits" beside the set. The set itself is the message.

### 12. Audience Persona Grid

**Message shape**: "For [audience A], [audience B], [audience C]."

**Picture**: 2-4 real-feel scenes or portraits, each clearly showing the audience in their natural context. The scenes differ enough to read as different lives.

**Examples**:
- Skincare: morning routine person, office worker, traveler, fitness person — each in a different small scene.
- Baby food: weaning parent, on-the-go parent, working parent.
- Earbuds: commuter, athlete, gamer, office worker.

**Product presence**: Variable. The audience identification is the point; product may appear small or be implied.

**Anti-template**: Do NOT use abstract persona icons (stick figures, role icons). Use real-feel mini-scenes or portraits.

## Pattern Combination Rule

A single screen may combine up to two patterns when the message genuinely requires both (e.g., "soft material" + "audience scene"). Combination is the exception, not the default. Most screens should pick exactly one pattern.

When combining, both patterns' product-presence rules apply — if either says "no packaging", packaging does not appear.

## Pattern Selection Hard Rule

If the model catches itself defaulting to "product hero centered" without first checking this library, that is a workflow violation. Stop, re-read the screen's message, find the matching pattern, and write the Picture Solo Statement based on the pattern's picture.

The pattern library is not optional. Skipping it is the most common root cause of 5-images-look-identical failure.
